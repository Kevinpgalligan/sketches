## Tiling theory
Regular tilings:
1. Every tile is a regular polygon.
  (Triangles, squares, hexagons)
2. Every tile is the same.
3. (Secret requirement) Sides line up exactly & all are the same size.

Semi-regular tilings (8 of them):
 Change requirement 2 so that every CORNER is the same.
 Can be constructed by applying symmetry-preserving operations to the regular tilings.
 E.g. TRUNCATION -- take a tiling and add shapes at the corners.
 New shapes need to be the right size so that the original shapes remain regular (i.e. all side lengths equal).
 Another e.g., RECTIFICATION, make new shapes big enough that original edges go away.

Laves tiling:
  Dual tiling by putting dots at centre of each shape, join adjacent shapes.
  Not very interesting for regular tilings (hexagon <-> triangle, square <-> square).
  Laves tilings are the duals of the semiregular tiles.
  Can be constructed by adding a new vertex to the centre of each shape and joining to the original vertices.

More tilings from combining the new shapes/tiles ("planigons") that result from Laves tilings.


Girih tiles are another option.
